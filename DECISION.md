## Why Kopi Shift wins my first build slot

You already care about this world and prefer the second idea. The central distinction is unusually economical: the language, object, decision, and payoff all occupy the same small counter. A correct action can be satisfying with no speech service, no account, and no navigation. That leaves time for the part people will notice—animation, composition, sound, and the stallholder's response.

The original brief has three orders, two modes, mentor questions and later tea/toast lessons. Keep that as the expansion map. For the first demo, build two cups beautifully. A third transfer order is useful only after the first two and recovery are dependable. Model-powered mentor questions are a possible addition, not the visual hook.

My creative direction would be **words you can pour**: an order ticket animates into a clear ingredient relationship; selecting a labelled pot visibly affects the cup; the omission of milk/sugar has a deliberate visual treatment; serving seals the order as a small souvenir. Do not put arbitrary words into the recipe, use implausible ingredient removal, or require players to perform an unnecessary stir.

The first 20 minutes of the chosen build should test the counter's defining gesture. Can an unfamiliar person pour into a cup and understand what changed without your narration? If not, refine that scene before adding customers or AI.

## When I would pick another direction

- **Pick Pocket Stage** if your excitement is specifically about a strange new physical feeling. The current pointer/keyboard toy already supports a short performance; a real hand-tracking version is a later stretch. Keep its original silhouette work clearly separate from claims of traditional puppetry.
- **Pick Living Tape** if the line “the tape can hear you” makes you want to direct the whole demo. It has the strongest narrative reveal, but the provided sketch uses explicitly simulated speech. Do not treat the sketch as evidence of a working voice service.
- **Pick Can Lah!** if you want the quickest, most graphic invitation to play. The visual language is strong enough to project well, but get a local speaker to shape the actual performances before making broad cultural claims.
- **Pick Table Chorus** if you want to invite another person into the presentation. Rehearse the invitation, then the reveal where the two cup rings and tapping marks become a keepsake portrait. That interaction is the concept's reason to exist.

## A build boundary that protects the magic

Choose one of these concepts. Give it one setting, one main gesture, one surprising consequence, and one ending. Use a title/setup overlay when required, then stay in the world. Storyboard frames are states within that world, not extra pages. A two-dimensional scene with well-directed motion can feel spatial and tactile; full 3D is optional.

For an assumed five focused build hours: spend the first hour proving the gesture and visual composition, the next two finishing a complete path, the fourth on recovery and sensory detail, and the last on rehearsal. Cut extra content before cutting the ending. This is an internal budget, not a published makeathon schedule.

The [organiser's brief](https://luma.com/yjffwqr2) supports experimental work and an open choice of medium. It does not publish judging weights. A compelling live performance is the thing we can control.
