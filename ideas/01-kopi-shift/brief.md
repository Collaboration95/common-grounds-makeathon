# Kopi Shift — Words you can pour

**Culture × Tech.** Run a tiny kopi counter. A written Singapore coffee order becomes something you can pick up, pour, stir and leave out. The pleasure is understanding a word through the consequence of your hands. Two cups share one patient counter; there is no queue, countdown or economy.

The exact magic moment: lift the silver **KOPI** pot over cup 01. **Kopi** detaches from its ticket and travels down into the coffee surface. Beside that cup, **Kosong** leaves a dashed sugar-cube-shaped space. Nothing falls into it. Its emptiness is the correct choice. For cup 02, **C** travels with the evaporated milk; stirring changes the surface into a pale spiral. The tickets remain readable throughout. The metaphor does not turn O into sugar or imply that Kosong means no milk.

Unlike the source's one customer and cup at a time, two cups are simultaneously present to make black/no-sugar versus milk/sugar a readable material contrast, as requested for this concept. Each cup has an independent validator. The minimum removes condensed milk as a distractor; it therefore **does not test the ability to distinguish two milk tins**. Do not describe that teaching goal as implemented. A later complete Kopi Shift build can restore the reviewed condensed-milk alternative.

## Three variants and the choice

1. **Word Cast Counter — recommended.** A paper diorama with labelled vessels, physically travelling order typography, and one empty sugar outline. A small DOM/SVG build can make the gesture feel authored and tactile without a physics engine.
2. **The Empty Ingredient.** An overhead letterpress counter: O and Kosong punch milk and sugar shapes out of a recipe stencil. Slide the stencil over the cup to inspect the unfilled holes. Absence is the main action, but this risks teaching a puzzle legend instead of everyday making.
3. **Little Morning Theatre.** A playful 2.5D counter with chunky porcelain cups and a narrow serving ledge. The whole order slip folds into a miniature cup stage after serving. Strong object appeal; weaker word/ingredient causality and substantially more art work.

Choose **Word Cast Counter**, with a single negative-space sugar outline supporting the same pour mechanic. Protect the split between O (no milk) and Kosong (no added sugar). The toy's charm is the copper-red ticket typography against a silver pot, cream porcelain and a deep teal bench; colour is reinforced by container shape, labels and persistent actual contents.

## Research and takeaways

References checked 11 October 2026; links are primary sources. These are precedents and terminology checks, not evidence that the proposed experience improves learning.

- [HealthHub — Getting Your Caffeine Hit](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/food-diet-and-nutrition/i-love-coffee-i-love-tea): its local coffee-shop table distinguishes O with sugar/no milk, C with evaporated milk/sugar, and Kosong with no sugar. Lock those ingredient categories in reviewed content. The toy uses ready-brewed coffee and symbolic additions, not ratios or dietary claims.
- [MIT Tangible Media Group — Tangible Bits](https://tangible.media.mit.edu/project/tangible-bits/): a precedent for connecting digital information to graspable objects. Our design takeaway is to make an order manipulable through its ingredient vessels. This browser sketch is screen-based; it is not a physical tangible interface or a new HCI invention.
- [State of Play — Lumino City and KAMI](https://www.stateofplaygames.com/games): Lumino City's handmade paper/card/wood miniature construction suggests visible thickness, directional shadows, and a coherent small world. KAMI's paper folding reinforces that the material can carry an action. Our SVG uses original silhouettes rather than borrowing game assets or reproducing their scenes.
- [Ben Esposito — Donut County](https://donutcounty.com/): the creator presents a physics puzzle centred on a hole. The relevant design inference is that negative space can be the memorable player-facing object. Our sugar absence is a static authored stencil; it does not use a physics hole or its story/world.

The [Common Ground event page](https://luma.com/yjffwqr2) is the source for the Culture × Tech fit and experimental medium. Judging weights and presentation duration remain unknown. A 90-second route and five-hour build are internal assumptions.

## Authored versus live

**Authored:** all customer request text, definitions, palettes, container shapes, sound tones, flying word paths, clarification sample and final response. No live voice, camera, generated customers, inference API or natural-language interpretation. An unclear milk sample is a deliberately triggered content branch, never a simulated microphone failure.

**Live:** pointer position, optional ingredient drag, click-then-cup input, keyboard shortcuts, actual cup contents, stirring state, guidance preference, sound preference and recipe validation. Pointer and keyboard share the same apply action. Animation is decoration rather than a success trigger; reduced motion reaches the same state immediately.

The travelling word is an extra typographic cast from the written order; the ticket remains a reference. Neither a decorative ghost sugar cube nor the word Kosong is added as a cup ingredient. Once poured, ingredient state is retained until an explicitly confirmed fresh cup.

## Risks and originality boundary

Coffee assembly, drag-and-drop, paper dioramas and word-to-object animation have precedents. The proposed synthesis is a Kopi Shift interpretation: grammar becomes material and omission gets a visible space. It is not claimed to be world-first, historically authentic, culturally comprehensive or likely to win.

Literal negative space can misteach: O alone does not remove sugar, and C does not mean condensed milk. Tickets spell out both distinctions; the optional O task makes the sugar contrast concrete. One symbolic addition teaches categories only. Cultural performance and future pronunciation need review from someone familiar with local ordering. The two simultaneous tickets are a comparison device, not a claim about stall workflow.

The current fixed composition is best on laptop/tablet. Small phones shrink text and targets; phone art direction remains a cut, rather than claiming full mobile accessibility. Keyboard controls and subtitles are implemented. Local file operation must be retained even when the live preview host is unavailable.

Live-board sync status, screenshots and persistence evidence are in `board.json` and `iteration-log.md`. The editable SDK export is `storyboard.snapshot.json`; it preserves work independently of remote sync.
