01
Closest to your dots idea

Dot — the living footnote

A small expressive dot carries a question to the exact on-page words or link that answer it, then anchors your local note there.

Target feeling: A little reader shares your attention; you see exactly where its answer comes from.

Drag Dot onto a paragraph, menu section, or captioned photo; ask one short question; follow its landing; select text to pin a note.

The moment worth showing

Dot leaves the terrace photo, rolls to the policy sentence, and replies beside the highlighted words; missing evidence brings it home.

  1. Drop Dot onto the restaurant terrace photo and ask: Smoking allowed? The caption sets context.
  2. Dot lands beside Smoking permitted only in the designated terrace area and preserves the qualification.
  3. On the unstated-policy variant, Dot returns to the photo: Page does not say. An outdoor photo is not permission.

Demo MVP · 5 focused hours

  • Toolbar-invoked unpacked MV3 extension with activeTab, scripting and storage; three localhost reference pages: /permitted.html, /prohibited.html, /unstated.html; reject unsupported pages.
  • Expressive 28-pixel draggable body; highlighted paragraph/menu/photo target; caption or alt text for photos; one question up to 160 characters; smoking and Book intents.
  • Scroll and travel to exact highlighted source text; adjacent reply up to 30 words preserving qualifications; distinguish absent evidence, unsupported question, and extraction failure.
  • One real constrained model call if credentials are ready: Ask previews explicitly selected context and local evidence, at most 1,200 characters; validate returned evidence ID and verbatim quote, then render a reviewed reply preserving qualifications; fixed code controls actions.
  • One editable local text-anchored note; URL, exact quote, and surrounding text stored locally; reload restore and scroll/resize positioning; missing anchor shows Original text changed.
  • Point to original Book anchor and destination; only the user's click opens /booking-info.html, the fictional booking-information landing page; no booking form interaction.

Outside this build

  • Universal sites, Gmail, PDFs, iframes, hidden content, image understanding, and dynamic-page anchor repair.
  • Chat history, multiple pets, proactive advice, general browsing, and cross-page research.
  • Completed bookings, form filling, messages, accounts, note sync, and publishing.

Hardest part: Stable source extraction, precise text anchoring, and legible travel across page layouts are harder than giving Dot personality; static fixtures do not establish arbitrary-site competence.

Fallback: Reviewed smoking/Book intents resolve deterministically to checked DOM quotes or reviewed page absence. Label Local evidence. Unsupported questions abstain; failed extraction or quote mismatch never means page does not say. Cut model integration after 45 minutes without a reliable round trip.

Recommend the living-footnote version: every answer has a landing place; missing evidence brings Dot home. Spend the five hours on pointing, evidence travel, one local pin, and the original Book link.

  • Chrome Extensions — activeTab — Observed documentation: explicit invocation grants temporary current-tab access; scripting allows injection; same-origin navigation can retain access. Design inference: enforce a separate three-page support boundary.
  • Chrome Extensions — Content scripts — Observed documentation: content scripts access page DOM from isolated JavaScript execution. Design inference: a resident overlay is plausible, but layout compatibility remains unproven.
  • W3C CSSOM View — Range.getClientRects — Observed specification: selected text has rectangles; returned geometry is not live. Design inference: exact landings and pins need recomputation after scroll or resize.

02
Most distinctive website gesture

Peel — the useful side of a website

Pull a website’s corner back to reveal its existing reservation doorway, field entrances and policy text, tethered to their origins.

Target feeling: Curiosity followed by relief: the useful bits were inside this object.

Drag one raised corner inward for a continuous reveal; release to latch open, reverse to close. Escape and keyboard equivalents remain available.

The moment worth showing

The original reservation label lifts from its outlined navigation source into the folded underside while a visible tether stays attached; closing retracts it.

  1. Pull the corner on Linen, a prepared fictional restaurant page; a shaded flap follows your hand while the original website stays visible.
  2. Choose Table for two; the reservation doorway, guest-field entrance and original booking condition gather underneath. Tap the entrance and manually choose two in the original selector.
  3. Reopen for literal smoking text and its source; refold to restore the page. Roof, a second prepared fixture, explicitly has no smoking policy to reveal.

Demo MVP · 5 focused hours

  • Exactly two local restaurant fixtures, Linen and Roof, with declared DOM selectors and disclosed prepared mapping; derive content from the page rather than hardcoded answers.
  • One continuously draggable corner, latched open state, reverse/Escape closure, keyboard equivalent and reduced-motion behavior.
  • Exactly two intent chips, Table for two and Smoking policy, with at most three revealed items; include literal booking conditions and a page-scoped missing-policy state.
  • Origin labels, tethers to visible source outlines or labelled offscreen edge markers, and Show original that closes, scrolls to and highlights the actual source node.
  • Preserve source link destinations; project fields only as entrances that focus native controls for manual edits. Never duplicate widgets or submit reservations.
  • Add only an overlay; retain source nodes and restore pre-open scroll/focus on closure except explicit origin handoff. Preserve native field edits.

0–10Pull Linen’s corner and show the reservation label rise from its outlined origin.
10–30Choose Table for two; show the tether and unchanged booking condition.

Outside this build

  • Arbitrary websites, third-party booking-widget reconstruction, availability checks or booking automation.
  • Summaries, chatbots, open-ended page questions or AI-generated policies.
  • Persistent redesign, blanket content stripping, accounts or saved workspaces.

Hardest part: Tactile motion needs early proof. Copying arbitrary interactive widgets may lose behavior; this MVP keeps native fields at their source and makes no broad compatibility claim.

Fallback: Replace realistic curl geometry with a triangular hinged flap; preserve continuous drag, source tethers, both fixtures and reversal.

Credible five-hour interaction demo with prepared mappings. Make the tether the central invention: each useful piece visibly remains attached to its website. Broad site compatibility remains unproven.

  • Chrome for Developers — Content scripts — Observed: content scripts inspect shared DOM while JavaScript environments are isolated. Inference: future extension projections are plausible, but copied widget behavior is not established.
  • Apple Support — Hide distractions when reading articles in Safari on Mac — Observed: Reader reformats supported articles without navigation distractions. Inference: Peel needs an action surface, source attachment and reversible movement to distinguish itself.
  • Dishoom — Bookings and Reservations — Observed: time/group booking restrictions and an external SevenRooms link appear on the page. Inference: reveal original conditions with the doorway; link extraction does not establish booking feasibility.

Best for making things

Web Magnets — Caption Split

Tear a phrase and photo from two pages into a floating margin; slide words through the photo to open a tiny poster with source threads.

Target feeling: I made those two things say something together: playful editorial surprise through spatial juxtaposition.

Drag a verbatim phrase across a photo's horizontal seam; attraction within 24 pixels previews the snap. Release to compose; pull the phrase away to unpair.

The moment worth showing

KEEP MOVING splits a snail photo into two intact halves around a white text band: an absurd motivational poster. TAKE YOUR TIME changes its meaning using the same rule.

  1. Select KEEP MOVING on the authored Instructions for a busy day fixture; pin the words into the margin.
  2. Pin the team's own snail photo from Things outside my window; one board holds three phrases and two photos.
  3. Snap words into the snail's seam, then unpair and swap to TAKE YOUR TIME; both sources remain attached.

Demo MVP · 5 focused hours

  • Two labelled localhost fixtures with three authored phrases and two team-owned photos: KEEP MOVING, TAKE YOUR TIME, DO NOT DISTURB, snail, spinning fan.
  • Explicit selection or image context-menu capture pins only the chosen item, with a lift animation; originals stay on the page.
  • One floating board shared across page invocations, local coordinates and provenance, maximum six inputs; five in the demo.
  • One physical snap grammar: separate intact image halves around a fitted verbatim text band, retain source tags, drag to unpair, one pair at a time.
  • Preserve exact text, owned asset ID, URL/title and anchor; a thin thread highlights a visible origin, while an off-page source uses a labelled return chip.
  • Live source return, phrase replacement after unpairing, and reset; both source tags remain visible on the composition.

15–30Switch to B, invoke the margin, and pin the snail; three preloaded fixture selections complete the five-item board.

Outside this build

  • Searchable archive, folders, accounts, or summariser.
  • AI rewriting or inferred relationships, multiple snap grammars, multi-image layouts, or typography editing.
  • Universal site support, arbitrary third-party artwork capture, or image/PDF export.

Hardest part: Reliable cross-page capture and source return within five hours; controlled anchors and owned assets bound the risk, while arbitrary web image retrieval is unproven.

Fallback: The same two fixture documents inside one local demo shell with a persistent margin and live capture/snapping; disclose simulated navigation and absent extension integration.

Recommend the bounded fixture version. Improve magnetic clippings by making words physically open an image to become its voice; the transformation earns the concept beyond a moodboard.

  • Chrome activeTab — Observed: user invocation grants temporary page access, revoked across origins. Inference: invoke on each source page and maintain board continuity separately in local state.
  • Chrome contextMenus — Observed: selectionText, pageUrl and srcUrl are supplied. Inference: text capture is straightforward; an image URL does not establish retrievable bytes or reuse permission, so map owned fixture assets.
  • Whitney Museum: John Baldessari — Observed: Whitney describes snapshots paired with didactic text and meaning changing with context. Inference: one verbatim imperative plus an incongruous owned photo can carry the demo; no artwork is copied.

Most sensory

Page Orchestra — Margin Sweep

Sweep down a webpage’s margin to hear its headings and text as a brief composition, with spacing, columns and images shaping the performance.

Target feeling: This familiar page was quietly a score.

After explicitly enabling sound, drag downward in the left margin; hand speed controls tempo. A focused Conduct button activated with Enter runs an eight-second sweep. Upward movement does not retrigger events.

The moment worth showing

A heading answers the hand with bass; changing to a two-column page creates simultaneous events and different rests under exactly the same rules.

  1. A fictional library guide loads muted: one title, two section headings, three paragraphs and a large photograph.
  2. Enable sound and sweep: headings give bass, paragraph punctuation gives soft taps, and the photograph holds a silent visual pad.
  3. Release to restore the page; switch to a two-column museum visit fixture and hear paired events, fewer taps and wider pauses.

Demo MVP · 5 focused hours

  • Use the same mapper on both structurally different fixtures: at most 16 visible h1/h2/p/img nodes inside main, with boxes clipped to the viewport. Heading centres trigger 350 ms triangle C2/G2 notes; up to four sentence-ending punctuation fractions per paragraph trigger 45 ms filtered-noise taps along its height, or one final tap without punctuation. Images are silent visual pads; gaps are rests; horizontal centres set modest stereo position.
  • Support pointer and keyboard sweeps with corresponding local outlines/underlines, once-only events per pass, and at most four simultaneous audio voices.
  • Start muted; explicit Enable sound resumes Web Audio. Provide volume, mute and Escape stop; synthesize locally with oscillator/noise and gain envelopes.
  • Use one removable SVG overlay, preserve source text/styles/links/scroll position, and clear the performance within 400 ms on release, completion or a ten-second timeout while silencing all tails.
  • Cancel on scroll/resize and remeasure on the next pass. Exclude navigation, forms and unsupported nodes.
  • Use gentle fades without flashes or strobe; provide static reduced-motion highlighting and a complete muted interaction.

Outside this build

  • AI music, speech or semantic content analysis.
  • Universal webpage support, embedded frames or complex app DOMs.
  • Recording, looping, sharing, camera gestures or instrument editing.

Hardest part: Geometry-driven timing may sound cluttered, and C2/G2 bass may disappear on laptop speakers. Audition first and reduce the node cap to eight if needed. Arbitrary-page extraction and cleanup remain unproven.

Fallback: Use the eight-second keyboard sweep over measured geometry if dragging fails. If venue audio fails, demonstrate muted mapping without treating it as proof of sound quality.

Recommend a controlled local stage with two fixtures, direct geometry mapping and reliable restoration. Extension integration earns its place later only if an unprepared public page is essential and the same mapper works on two agreed pages with clean removal and no site-specific selectors; use user-invoked activeTab/scripting injection. Sharpen the seed by making whitespace and column alignment audible.

  • MDN — Web Audio API best practices — Observed: oscillator/buffer synthesis, user-gesture context startup, audio controls and gain scheduling. Design inference: a gentle, bounded local sound palette.
  • MDN — Element.getBoundingClientRect() — Observed: viewport-relative element geometry changes with scrolling. Design inference: vertical spacing becomes rests, alignment becomes simultaneous events, and scroll cancels the snapshot.
  • Chrome for Developers — scripting API — Observed: scripting plus activeTab permits temporary host access and script injection defaults to an isolated world. Design inference: user-invoked overlay integration is a later step; cleanup reliability still needs validation.

Strongest playful experiment

Cursor Choir — catch your past hand

Record a gesture, cross its ghost's bright moving tail to pluck it, then tug time until three past hands answer each other.

Target feeling: My earlier hand is still here, and I can play with it; an instant self-duet.

Hold to record up to four seconds; release to replay. Cross another ghost's bright tail; tug its origin time knot horizontally to shift phase.

The moment worth showing

The same two paths first miss, then pluck each other after one time tug; the receiving ribbon and ghost rebound.

  1. Draw and release a two-second sideways arc; a numbered ghost repeats immediately.
  2. Draw through its bright tail; the earlier ribbon and ghost rebound. Release take two.
  3. Tug phase to restore an encounter; add take three yourself or let an audience member contribute, then frame the motion postcard.

Demo MVP · 5 focused hours

  • Capture held pointer positions and timestamps for up to four seconds; release starts replay while another take can be recorded.
  • Three numbered ghosts with faint full paths and bright 0.8-second tails; four-second laps preserve recorded speed and pauses, wait at endpoint, and fade at wrap.
  • Live or replayed moving segments pluck another take only at its bright tail; no self-crossings, wrap collisions or repeated contact chatter.
  • Receiving ribbon and ghost visibly rebound for about 250 ms; origin time knots scrub and retain phase without changing paths.
  • Undo-last, restart and pointer-cancellation recovery.
  • Frame a repeating four-second motion postcard with Replay/Restart on the same canvas.

Outside this build

  • No extension, external click replay, accounts, camera or model; dedicated canvas only.
  • No timeline editor, extra layers, physical string simulation, variable loop lengths or speed stretching.
  • No sound, media export, cloud sharing or simultaneous multiplayer; audience contributes through the same pointer.

Hardest part: Main uncertainty is whether bright-tail encounters and phase-induced changes read clearly among overlapping paths; prove one live pluck in hour one.

Fallback: Widen bright tails from 0.8 to 1.4 seconds and use a rehearsed central arc/cross. Preserve conditional plucking; trails alone fail the concept.

Recommend if the first-hour pluck reads clearly. Core improvement: faded-path crossings do nothing, so time determines whether your past hand answers.

  • W3C Pointer Events — Observed: capture redirects subsequent pointer events; cancellation ends streams. Inference: handle cancellation explicitly for reliable held gestures.
  • MDN requestAnimationFrame — Observed: callbacks provide timestamps, follow display refresh and commonly pause in background tabs. Inference: elapsed-time playback with pause/rebase on hide.
  • Richard C. Davis: K-Sketch, UC Berkeley — Observed: pen input demonstrates animation motion. Inference: record/replay alone is insufficient; conditional encounters make time the material. No novelty claim.

My recommendation

Start with Dot.
Give it a physical verb.

Drag it onto something. Let it lead you to evidence. Pin it as a note. The character's relationship with the page is the design.

If you want a more playful departure, try Cursor Choir: a duet with your own past gestures. Peel has the strongest website-transformation moment, with a tighter supported-page boundary.
