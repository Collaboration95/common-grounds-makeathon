# The Tape Can Hear You

**Culture × Tech. Recommended variant: The Second Take.** An old fictional welcome programme is waiting for your answer. You name a dish; a tray lifts, a hand follows, a portion arcs toward the plate. Change your mind and the tape physically reverses that little performance. The ending is not a lesson completed. It is your friend pulling up the seat you kept for her.

One stall. One friend, Mei. One shared order: a plate of rice with either char siew or roast pork. Mei's note says, “Save me a seat. Order a plate of rice. We'll share whatever you pick.” There is no hidden correct answer. The player shapes lunch rather than passes a vocabulary test. The theatrical fiction explains both the listening and the impossible undo: this is an authored welcome tape, not archival footage or an actual food order.

The exact magic moment is **“Actually, char siew.”** The roast-pork portion travels back along its curved flight, the rice disappears back into the scoop, the order slip unwrites, and the tray settles. Then the char-siew tray wakes and the same choreography plays forward. The apparent recording has become responsive. Correction makes the tape premise do work rather than serve as a filter.

## Relationship to the originals

Idea 1 supplied the speaking customer, fictional VHS welcome tape, friendly direct address, raised named dish, paper friend message and preparation consequence. This keeps all six and reduces its two stalls, multiple orders, host and teaching recap to one sustained scene. The project audit correctly demanded a fictional programme, matching dining container, explicit microphone consent, directed recovery and a real shared-lunch payoff. We adopt those recommendations and make the empty seat visible before the order; the final hand, second pair of chopsticks and moving stool resolve it.

The new `design-idea-2.md` establishes Kopi Shift as the active original direction. It remains a **Culture** idea: the player hears an order and performs the work of choosing ingredients and making a drink. This alternate concept does not supersede that preference. It borrows Kopi Shift's tangible consequences, stable object positions, deterministic state and complete ending, but changes agency: the customer conducts a small impossible performance with words. Realistic mixed-cup reset belongs to Kopi Shift; physically reversing props here is deliberately fictional. No claim of historical authenticity or educational superiority follows.

The supplied walking sprites are reference for warmth and stepped performance, not imported assets. Walking poses would add motion without serving this counter exchange. The preview uses original editable SVG geometry, a glasses-wearing stallholder and one reaching arm; character delivery needs local review.

## Three variants, then a choice

| Variant | Distinct interaction and mood | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Held Breath | Name a dish to release a frozen welcome programme. Everything resumes together: uncle, tray, lamp and lunch. | Immediate reveal; weaker second beat and less reason to retain tape controls. |
| **The Second Take** | Say a dish, then a correction. Reverse the serving props and replay the corrected order. End by saving the seat. | Best two-beat performance; requires a reversible draft timeline and clear commitment boundary. |
| The Last Seat | The tape responds only when the player says “Save me a seat.” A second place appears and a forgotten lunch resumes. | More intimate fiction, but dish vocabulary and material food consequence become incidental. |

Choose **The Second Take**. It has a reveal, an escalation and an emotional landing in the same composition. Do not add a second stall or a menu adventure to deepen it.

## Researched precedents

Research checked 11 October 2026. These are interaction references, not assets to reproduce or claims of invention.

1. [Nintendo Labo, Nintendo's original announcement](https://www.nintendo.com/en-za/News/2018/January/Nintendo-Labo-combines-fun-interactive-make-play-and-discover-experiences-with-Nintendo-Switch-1328999.html) describes cardboard constructions whose physical inputs animate virtual consequences, including an IR-sensed piano. **Takeaway:** humble, legible materials make sensing feel magical. Show a tray and food moving in response, rather than a transcript or AI dashboard. Our screen-only props do not reproduce Labo hardware.
2. [Connected Worlds, Design I/O](https://www.design-io.com/projects/connectedworlds) describes visitors diverting water with physical logs and planting with their hands; changes propagate through the installation's habitats. **Takeaway:** an input is persuasive when consequences continue through the world. Here a dish name causes tray → hand → food → plate, not only a selected label. Our finite choreography is much smaller than its ecosystem.
3. [Hawker Culture, National Heritage Board / Roots](https://www.roots.gov.sg/ich-landing/ich/hawker-culture) frames dining and mingling at hawker centres as everyday Singapore life. Search text was available; the direct page presented a verification gate when opened. **Takeaway:** companionship is integral, so the saved seat and shared meal carry the Culture framing. Do not reduce hawker culture to a dictionary or one stereotyped character.
4. [UNESCO's Singapore nomination document](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/44410-EN.pdf) describes hawker centres as communal places where diverse people dine and mingle. **Takeaway:** finish with two places around one plate, not a mastery score. It supports the social framing; it does not certify our stall illustration or fictional tape.

The [event page](https://luma.com/yjffwqr2), as verified in the supplied creative direction, accepts open mediums and speculative prototypes in Culture × Tech. ## One-screen anatomy

A sage-green illustrated hawker stall sits inside a warm, rounded CRT shell. A paper sign, two distinct named trays, uncle, and Mei's note anchor the top half. The table below holds a blank order slip, one plate, the player's chopsticks and an empty coral stool. The visible table is already the ending's stage; the camera never changes location. The outer tape deck contains simulated phrase keys and a reversible timeline slider. The scene has only a setup overlay and an ending overlay. Subtitles, sound, reduced motion and reset stay available. Grain is static and scanlines are subtle; no strobe or continuous glitch.

One word activates one object chain. The tray and label keep their positions, so correction has a visible origin and destination. Colour is secondary to shape and labels. The second hand crosses from beyond the television edge, suggesting somebody has joined the same tiny world rather than opened another screen.

## Authored and live boundaries

**Delivered preview:** dependency-free SVG/CSS/JavaScript, usable via `file://`. Every phrase is explicitly labelled **SIMULATED SPEECH INPUT · NO MICROPHONE**. No speech APIs, capture, network requests, transcript model, fake waveform, or hidden live sensing. The user genuinely controls dish choice, reverse choreography, the prop slider, ambiguity, sound, reduced motion, commitment and replay. Optional audio consists of small synthesized tones, not voiced dialogue. The script parser is only button dispatch.

**Planned actual product:** setup copy: “This tape listens only when you press Answer. Your answer is sent to [the selected speech provider] to recognise your words. We discard our recording after processing. The provider's retention policy is [verified policy/link].” Buttons: “Enable microphone” and “Play without microphone.” Replace bracketed values before shipping; they are unresolved production dependencies. Enable triggers browser permission; mere Play never does. Default is Mic off. Answer opens a maximum six-second turn with “Listening · Stop & send”; stop, timeout or Cancel closes capture. “Understanding…” then validates a supported dish/correction before motion. Mic off during playback and after each answer; Stop listening and ending/reset release tracks. Keep typed input and named dish buttons available without permission.

Speech transcription and constrained intent interpretation may be live. Dish identities, response lines, cultural content, movement, serving, and ending are authored. Validate `{intent, dish, rejectedDish, unresolved}` against the two-item menu. Never infer “that one,” silently replace an unsupported dish, treat a speech failure as a wrong answer or expose unrestricted generated dialogue. Network/permission failure preserves the draft and offers typing. Give the interpreter a revision token; ignore late replies after reset. An actual validated correction cancels the active draft timeline, returns props to the neutral pose, then replays the corrected dish. Save the seat commits once. After the shared-lunch ending, Replay begins a new fictional take; it does not un-serve a real meal.

They are the reason to build this direction.

## Risks and honest originality boundary

Listening media, responsive scenes, named food choices, retro TV styling and reversible animation all have precedents. The proposed contribution is their specific combination: a fictional Singapore welcome tape whose order correction physically rewinds a shared-lunch performance. No world-first or winning claim. Culture framing comes from an everyday social exchange and a place kept for a friend, not a promise to teach or assimilate the user.

Recognition of char siew, interruptions, venue noise and latency are untested in this toy. Push-to-talk turns avoid continuous barge-in engineering. Corrections operate before commitment; the actual product must reject unsupported requests and deduplicate turns. Food proportions and the single shared plate are theatrical conventions. One fictional stall does not represent every community, diet or ordering practice; the illustration and recorded local delivery need review. The board's browser route was editable and visually inspected; persistence and snapshot status are recorded separately in `board.json` and QA evidence rather than assumed from the presence of a URL.
